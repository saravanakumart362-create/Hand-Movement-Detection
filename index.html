The first button is the caregiver button wired to pin D2. It silences an alert and pauses monitoring for 30 seconds, so attending the patient does not set the alarm off.

Try this

  1. Play Finger twitch. The alert fires within a fraction of a second and clears 3 seconds after the hand is still.
  2. Play Faint finger flex. It is missed. Lower the rotation threshold to 1.0 °/s and play it again.
  3. Raise Sensor noise to 6 × typical. False alerts start. Raise the thresholds until they stop.
  4. Drag the hand to one side and hold it there. The alert clears, because a tilted hand that is still is not moving.
  5. Play Tremor, then Wrist turn. The log names the first Shaking and the second a Long movement, and the bigger one beeps faster.

What the glove can and cannot tell you

    

      
It detects motion, not meaning
An alert says the hand moved. It cannot say whether the movement was voluntary, a reflex, or a sign of returning consciousness. A caregiver still has to assess the patient.

      
Small finger movements can slip through
A sensor on the back of the hand feels a finger flex only faintly. Play Faint finger flex, then lower the rotation threshold until it is caught. The sketch also reads a second sensor on a finger, at address 0x69.

      
Outside movement looks the same
A bumped bed or a caregiver adjusting the glove trips the alert too. Play Bed bump to see it, and use the caregiver button while attending the patient.

      
A prototype, not a medical device
Clinical use would need controlled testing, validated detection accuracy, proper alarm handling and a medical safety evaluation.
